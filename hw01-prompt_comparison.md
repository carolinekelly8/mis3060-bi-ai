1. What are the most important metrics for measuring credit risk in a consumer loan portfolio?
Response: Credit risk in a consumer loan portfolio is usually tracked through a mix of backward-looking (what's already happened) and forward-looking (what's likely to happen) metrics. Here's how they break down:

Delinquency and default metrics

Delinquency rates by bucket (30/60/90+ days past due) — the earliest warning signal of portfolio deterioration
Roll rates — the percentage of accounts that move from one delinquency bucket to a worse one (e.g., 30→60 days). This shows momentum, not just a snapshot
Default rate — share of loans that cross the contractual definition of default (often 90+ DPD or charge-off)
Early payment default (EPD) — loans that default within the first few months of origination, which usually points to underwriting or fraud issues rather than macro conditions 

2. You are a senior credit risk analyst presenting to a portfolio committee of three senior executives...What are the most important metrics for measuring credit risk in a consumer loan portfolio?
Response: Good framing — here's how I'd structure this for a portfolio committee, organized by what each category of metric tells you.

Loss & Charge-Off Metrics (the bottom line)
Net Charge-Off Rate (NCO) — annualized charge-offs net of recoveries, divided by average outstanding balance. The single most-watched number in the room.
Gross Charge-Off Rate — before recoveries; useful for isolating collections performance.
Recovery Rate — how much of charged-off balances get clawed back, and how fast.
Loss Given Default (LGD) — expected loss severity if a loan defaults, critical for secured products (auto, mortgage, HELOC).

3. Answer in 3 high level bullet points. What are the most important metrics for measuring credit risk in a consumer loan portfolio?
Response: Delinquency and default rates – metrics like 30/60/90+ day delinquency rates, charge-off rate, and probability of default (PD) show what share of borrowers are falling behind or failing to repay.
Loss severity and exposure – loss given default (LGD) and exposure at default (EAD) capture how much is actually lost when a borrower defaults, factoring in recoveries and collateral.
Portfolio quality and concentration – metrics like weighted-average credit score (e.g., FICO), debt-to-income ratios, vintage curves, and concentration by segment (geography, loan type, borrower risk tier) reveal underlying risk trends and diversification.
